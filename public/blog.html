FAITH / PERSONAL ESSAY

Why Jesus Is God

Learning from the Master.

Written by Arayan Lama

I was once lost, but not found.

I was once just a man, but now I'm a man saved by grace.

I fell into cycles of anxiety and isolation. I struggled with an addiction to pornography and found myself constantly seeking satisfaction through girls and romantic relationships.

Nothing was enough! No person could fill the hole in my heart—a space that only God could fill.

I kept searching for something that would make me feel complete. Something that would take away the emptiness, something that would give me a reason to feel satisfied.

But the more I searched for satisfaction in people and temporary pleasures, the more I began to question what I was actually looking for.

Was it love? Was it acceptance? Was it companionship? Or was I expecting another person to give me something they were never meant to provide?

I began to understand an important distinction: being loved by another person and finding my worth in another person are not the same thing.

People can love us, care for us, and walk alongside us. Relationships can be beautiful, meaningful, and worth cherishing. But I don't believe another human being can carry the responsibility of making us whole.

That's too much to ask of anyone.

And perhaps that's what I had been doing—looking for something eternal in things that were temporary.

I believe that the emptiness in the human heart points toward something greater than ourselves.

For me, that answer is God.

Not because people are incapable of loving us, but because I believe we were created for a relationship with Him.

And this is where my understanding of Jesus begins.

Jesus doesn't simply offer another temporary source of satisfaction. He invites us into a relationship with God.

He doesn't ask us to pretend that we're perfect before approaching Him.

He calls the weary, the burdened, and those who need rest.

MATTHEW 11:28–29 · NLT
Then Jesus said, “Come to me, all of you who are weary and carry heavy burdens, and I will give you rest. Take my yoke upon you. Let me teach you, because I am humble and gentle at heart, and you will find rest for your souls.”

What stands out to me is that Jesus doesn't only promise rest. He also says, Let me teach you.

He calls us to learn from Him.

And that's what I want my faith to be about.

Not simply believing in Jesus when I need something, but learning from Him in the way I live, the way I love, and the way I treat others.

Because if Jesus is my Master, then I must also be willing to become His student.

I am not greater than my Master. I am here to learn from Him.

And perhaps becoming a man saved by grace is not about having every answer or never struggling again.

Perhaps it begins with recognizing that I cannot save myself.

That I need grace.

That I need God.

And that I still have much to learn.

I was once lost, but not found.

But my story doesn't have to end there.

Because only He—Jesus—can free you from your chains.
